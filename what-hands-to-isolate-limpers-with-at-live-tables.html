What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

How to identify which hands have enough equity and fold equity to iso-raise profitably, and why opponent type shifts your range.

By RangeIQ Poker · September 2026 · 5 min read

When a player limps in front of you at a $1/$3 table, you know they are not holding a premium. The real question is which hands in your range can convert that limp into an immediate advantage — and which ones just bloat a pot you are not equipped to win. Not every hand justifies an isolation raise. Knowing which ones do is one of the clearest edges you can build at live cash games.

Which Hands Qualify for an Isolation Raise

The baseline requirement for isolating limpers is: enough equity against the limper's likely range combined with enough fold equity from players still to act behind you. At $1/$3, most limpers are Loose Passive, Recreational, or Calling Station types. None of them fold to a reasonable raise preflop — so your fold equity comes primarily from squeezing out the players behind, not the limper themselves.

That changes your hand selection. Against opponents who call off $15–$20 preflop with any two suited cards or any pair, your iso-raising range should tilt heavily toward hands that dominate. Strong broadway combinations — A-K, A-Q, A-J, K-Q suited — big pairs (JJ+, and TT at reasonable depths), and connected high-card hands with position all belong here.

Speculative hands like 7♦6♦ or small pocket pairs like 4-4 are marginal in this spot. They need multiple callers and a cheap price to see a flop. When you iso-raise 7♦6♦ to $18 with two players behind, you have turned a drawing hand into a semi-bluff with unfavorable pot odds on most runouts. The hand works better completing in a multi-way pot, not building a heads-up pot out of position.

IQ Reasoning: A♥J♠ has strong domination equity against the UTG limper's typical call range (A2o–A9o, all pairs below TT, suited connectors). With three limpers, BTN position, and two players behind, a raise to $21 builds a profitable pot and eliminates speculative hands from proceeding cheaply. The objective is to reach the flop heads-up or against at most one opponent from a position of strength. Checking back or completing forfeits leverage entirely — flat-calling a limped pot with A♥J♠ from BTN produces a multi-way hand where top pair becomes difficult to extract value from and easy to misread.

Sizing: Why the Standard Formula Works

The common guideline at live games is to raise to 3x the big blind, plus one big blind for each limper. At $1/$3 with two limpers, that is 3 × $3 + 2 × $3 = $15. This sizing achieves two things: it prices out speculative hands from the players behind, and it forces the limper to commit $12–$15 more to continue.

Do not go smaller trying to "induce" a call. A $10 raise into a $1/$3 game with multiple limpers in front gets flat-called by half the table. You want to reach the flop as the clear aggressor against one opponent, not in a five-way pot where your position advantage shrinks to almost nothing. At $2/$5 with multiple limpers, apply the same formula — expect to raise to $25–$35 depending on limper count and table activity.

Sizing rule: 3x the big blind + 1 big blind per limper. Adjust up by $5 at active tables where this amount routinely gets multiple callers.

How Opponent Type Changes Your Iso Range

Against a Nit who limps early position, tighten significantly. A Nit limp in EP is often a small or medium pair set-mining, or occasionally a slow-play with a big hand. Iso-raising with A-J or K-Q is reasonable; iso-raising with 9♣8♣ is not, because the Nit is not folding and you are likely out of position against a player holding a pair you are behind.

Against a Maniac who limps, the calculus flips. Almost any strong hand should iso-raise Maniacs, because they call and then barrel aggressively on favorable and unfavorable runouts alike. Build bigger pots with your strong holdings against them. The danger is isolating a Maniac with a marginal hand — they will three-bet or call and force you to play a large pot in a difficult spot.
