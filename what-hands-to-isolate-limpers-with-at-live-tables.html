What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Isolation raising is one of the highest-leverage preflop spots in live cash — but only when you're targeting the right hands against the right opponent types.

By RangeIQ Poker · August 2026 · 5 min read

When a player limps into the pot, they're signaling one thing clearly: they have no preflop plan. At $1/$3 and $2/$5 live cash tables, limpers are the most reliable source of profit — but isolating them profitably requires picking the right hands, not just any hand that beats a limp.

The Hands That Print Against Limpers

The purpose of an isolation raise is twofold: take the pot preflop when everyone folds, or go to the flop as the range-advantage player with position. Both outcomes require holding hands that either fold out dead money reliably or extract value from weaker ranges postflop.

At most live tables, a solid iso range from late position includes: all pairs (22+), Broadway hands (AJ+, KQ, KJs, QJs), and high-equity suited connectors (T9s, 98s, 87s). Against a single limper from middle or late position, this range is narrow enough to maintain a genuine equity edge while remaining wide enough to attack limping ranges at an appropriate frequency.

The hands that don't belong in an iso range: suited aces below A8s from early position, offsuit connectors, and low pairs when there are multiple limpers or a likely squeeze from behind. These hands face multiway pots and lack the raw equity or bluff equity to make iso-raising profitable against calling-station populations.

Adjusting by Opponent Type

The limper's opponent type changes which part of your iso range you emphasize — it does not change the core range itself.

Against a Calling Station: lean on value. They call too wide preflop and postflop, so suited connectors with speculative equity lose value — you need hands that can bet three streets. Tighten toward QQ+, AK, AQ, AJs, KQs, and high pairs. Bluff-heavy iso hands (small suited connectors, low pairs in position) lose fold equity against a player who simply does not fold.

Against a Loose Passive: you can open the range slightly. Their postflop tendencies — checking and calling rather than raising — improve your implied odds on drawing hands. Hands like T9s, 98s, and small pairs go up in value when you can reliably see cheap turns and rivers. Still avoid pure bluff iso raises; they call too much preflop to make those profitable.

Against a Nit who has limped in early position: this is an unusual signal. Nits rarely limp strong hands from early position — it's typically a speculative hand they're trapping with or a genuine mistake. If there's no history suggesting a limp-reraise tendency, iso raise your full range. If they've shown a limp-reraise pattern before, tighten to hands that can call a 3-bet profitably.

Sizing the Iso-Raise at Live Stakes

Live game iso-raise sizing runs larger than online because the calling frequencies are higher and effective stacks are typically 100–200 big blinds. The standard framework: raise to 4–5x the big blind, then add 1 big blind per additional limper.

At $1/$3: one limper means raising to $15–18. Two limpers means $18–21. At $2/$5: one limper means raising to $25–30. Going below $12 at $1/$3 gives the limper correct pot odds to call with suited connectors and speculative hands — that's the opposite of what you want when you hold a premium. Going significantly larger than $20 at $1/$3 accomplishes little beyond folding out the exact players you most want to play against.
