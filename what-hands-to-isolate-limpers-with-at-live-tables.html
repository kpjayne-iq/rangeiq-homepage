What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Your iso-raise range is determined by the limper you're targeting and the opponent types still to act behind you — not a static preflop chart.

By RangeIQ Poker · August 2026 · 5 min read

Isolating limpers is one of the highest-edge spots in a $1/$3 live game, but most players work from a static range that ignores the most important variable: who is in the hand. The right iso-raise hands depend on the limper's tendencies, the opponent types remaining in the blinds, how many players have limped, and your position. This article breaks each factor down so you can study the right adjustments between sessions and apply them confidently at the table.

Position and Range Shape: The Baseline

When you iso-raise a single limper, you are building a pot heads-up against a player who has already signaled weakness. From late position (CO or BTN), your iso range is widest: pairs from 77 up, strong Broadway combinations (ATo+, KQo, KJo), and suited connectors with good equity and playability (JTs, T9s, 98s, A9s+, KTs+, QJs). These hands play well in position, extract value from dominated calls, and benefit from a pot size that rewards aggressive postflop play.

From early or middle position against a single limper, narrow to hands that are strong enough to play comfortably when called by a wider range in a larger pot: AA, KK, QQ, JJ, TT, AKs, AQs, AJs, KQs. The further you are from the button, the more players can wake up behind you with a re-raise, and the less equity you gain from position postflop.

One critical rule that most players violate: isolating into multiple limpers is not isolation. When four players have limped, a raise does not reliably remove them from the pot. You are inflating the price of a multi-way pot with a hand that often has marginal multi-way equity. Against two or more limpers, tighten to premium value hands only, or call along with speculative hands that benefit from multi-way implied odds — suited aces, small pairs, connected suited hands.

Opponent Type Reshapes the Calculation

The limper's opponent type determines whether your iso range leans toward value, draws, or neither. Against a Loose Passive or Calling Station limper, value hands go up in priority and pure bluff-equity hands go down. A Calling Station calls a $12 iso-raise with any pair, any Broadway, any suited hand. That is excellent for ATo, KJo, and even QTo — hands that flop top pair-top kicker against a dominated caller. Suited connectors lose relative value because a Calling Station's calling range is so wide that you rarely fold out the equity you need to fold out.

Against a Nit who limps, treat it as a potential trap: Nits limp AA and KK at live tables far more often than solvers suggest, and their limping range is weighted toward strong, disguised hands. Reduce your iso frequency with marginal holdings, and only raise with hands that retain good equity even if called by a strong range — AK, AQs, JJ+.

Against a Recreational player who limps, almost any decent hand becomes profitable to isolate. Recreational players call your raise with a wide, dominated range (K3o, 74s, any pair) and play poorly postflop. A hand like A5s or 65s that you might fold against a tight table is a solid iso-raise against a Recreational, because their calling range hands you both equity and EV through dominated pair equity and positional advantage postflop.

Sizing and the Number of Limpers

Your iso-raise size is not fixed. At $1/$3, a standard single-limper iso is $12–$15 (4x–5x the big blind plus $1 per limper). When two players have limped, size up to $15–$18 to reduce the number of callers. The goal is to play heads-up or at worst two-way. If three or more players have limped, fold or limp behind with speculative hands — even AA can warrant a large raise ($20–$25) to avoid a five-way pot where your equity advantage shrinks dramatically.
