What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Your iso-raise range is not fixed — it changes based on stack depth, position, and the type of limper you are targeting.

By RangeIQ Poker · October 2026 · 5 min read

At most $1/$3 and $2/$5 tables, limping is the default for a large portion of the field. Three players limp, you look down at J♥9♥ on the button, and the question is not whether to raise — it is whether that hand is strong enough to warrant raising, and how much. The answer depends on three variables: your position, the effective stack depth, and the type of player you are targeting. Getting this right is one of the highest-leverage preflop adjustments a live cash player can make.

The Core Iso-Raise Range by Position

Position is the first filter. From the button or cutoff, your iso-raise range is considerably wider than from early position, because you will play the rest of the hand in position against a player who has already signaled weakness by limping.

From the button, a standard iso-raise range at $1/$3 includes: all pocket pairs, all broadway hands (A-x, K-x, Q-x down to Q9s), suited aces (A2s through A9s), and suited connectors down to 65s. That is a wide range — roughly 25% of hands — because you have maximum positional advantage and the limper's range is capped by their passive action.

From early or middle position, tighten to hands that play well in a multi-way pot and have strong equity when called. Pairs, AK, AQs, KQs, and suited broadway hands do the job. J9s from UTG in a seven-handed limped pot is a trap: you will often flop a weak draw and face a bet from out of position with no clear line.

How Stack Depth Changes the Calculation

At $1/$3 with effective stacks of $200 (roughly 66 big blinds), implied odds are limited. This makes high-card hands more valuable relative to speculative ones. Raise KQo, AJs, and pocket tens aggressively. Small pocket pairs (22–55) lose value because the implied odds rarely justify the investment — a passive player who folds to any postflop pressure removes the stack depth you need to make small pairs profitable.

At $2/$5 with $500+ effective stacks (100 big blinds and deeper), the calculation shifts. Small pairs and suited connectors gain value because the implied odds justify the preflop price. A $20 iso-raise with 66 against a Loose Passive player sitting on $600 is correct. When you flop a set, you stack them. When you miss, you give up cleanly or take a small c-bet stab in position.

IQ Reasoning: JTs is a strong iso-raise candidate from the button against a Loose Passive opponent. The hand makes strong top pairs, straights, and flushes, and all of those hands extract value postflop. Raising to $15 denies the BB a free look, builds a pot you will play in position, and puts the limper in a marginal spot with a wide, weak range. A flat call here surrenders the initiative and invites multiway action that dilutes your edge. The sizing of $15 (5x) accounts for the limper already in the pot — standard is add one big blind per limper beyond the first.

Adjusting for the Limper's Opponent Type

The final variable is who you are targeting. Isolating a Nit who limps only with small pairs and suited connectors is a different spot than isolating a Recreational player who limps any two suited cards. Against the Nit, your iso-raise bluffing frequency should be low — they call or 3-bet with a genuinely strong hand, and their calling range postflop is tight and defensively sound. You want to be raising value hands that can withstand a call.

Against Calling Station and Recreational opponents, iso-raising with a wider range of strong value hands becomes more profitable because they call and call again on later streets. Hands like ATo, KJs, and QTs go up in value. Thin bluffs go down — a player who never folds does not respond to the raise as a fold-equity play, so the bluffing component of your range loses its effectiveness. You are building a pot to extract value, not applying pressure.
