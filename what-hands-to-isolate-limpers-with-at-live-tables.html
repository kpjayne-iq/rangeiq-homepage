What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Your iso-raise range isn't fixed — it shifts with position, limper count, and the opponent types already in the pot.

By RangeIQ Poker · August 2026 · 5 min read

Most players at $1/$3 treat isolating limpers like a two-step decision: good hand, raise; bad hand, fold. That framing ignores the three variables that actually determine your iso-raise range — position, how many players limped, and who specifically limped. Get those wrong and you're either leaving money on the table by playing too tight, or bloating pots out of position with hands that don't perform well multi-way.

The Baseline Iso Range: What Works in Position

The baseline iso range from the button or cutoff against a single Recreational or Loose Passive limper is wider than most players realize. Hands like A5s–A2s, KTs+, QJs, JTs, 65s–98s, and all pairs 22+ are standard iso candidates when you're last to act or close to it. These hands benefit from the inflated pot and perform significantly better heads-up than in a four-way limped pot where your equity advantage shrinks.

Out of position, the range contracts considerably. Iso-raising from UTG+1 into an early-position limper means you'll face more callers and play every street first. In that spot, tighten to hands with strong standalone equity: AJo+, KQo+, TT+, and suited broadways. Speculative hands like 54s or 77 don't generate enough value when the pot is multi-way and you're acting first on every street without the benefit of position.

How Opponent Type Changes Your Iso Decision

Against a Nit, the math shifts immediately. A Nit limping UTG at $1/$3 has a capped range — medium pairs, suited connectors, and the occasional broadway hand they didn't want to raise. Because Nits fold to preflop pressure at a high rate, fold equity becomes part of your expected value calculation. Your iso range widens and sizing can be larger. Even a hand like K9o becomes viable from the button when the Nit is the only limper — the equity from the high fold frequency covers the equity deficit of the weaker hand.

Against a Calling Station, the calculation flips. Calling Stations don't fold to preflop raises, which eliminates fold equity entirely. Your iso range should compress to hands with strong raw equity: TT+, AQo+, AJs+. You still isolate — the inflated pot is valuable — but you're doing it purely for post-flop value, not to steal. Iso-raising K9o into a player who calls every raise and chases every draw is a losing play over time.

A two-thirds pot bet extracts value from all weaker top-pair hands and charges any gut-shots or backdoor draws they may be holding. Checking risks giving a free card to a hand that would fold to a bet and forfeits pot control on a board where you are far ahead.

Sizing and Range Compression With Multiple Limpers

One limper and three limpers are fundamentally different spots. Against a single limper, a 4x open ($12 at $1/$3) is standard. Against two limpers, add one big blind per additional limper: 5x–6x ($15–$18). Against three or more, your range should compress to hands that play well multi-way — TT+, AQs+, and suited aces with strong blockers — because more callers reduce the probability you take the pot preflop and increase the importance of raw equity when called in multiple directions.

Stack depth also adjusts the calculation. When facing a deep-stacked limper (150bb+), speculative hands like 55 or T9s gain value because implied odds on set or two-pair boards increase. Against a short-stacked limper under 40bb, those same hands lose value — tighten to holdings that perform well in a low-SPR pot where stack-to-pot ratio compresses post-flop play.
