What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

The exact hands worth raising, the sizing that punishes limpers, and how opponent type changes both.

By RangeIQ Poker · September 2026 · 5 min read

At most $1/$3 and $2/$5 tables, limping is endemic. Two or three players see the flop for $3 before the action reaches you, and you have to decide whether to raise, call, or fold. The question isn't whether isolating limpers is correct in principle — it almost always is from late position — but which hands are actually worth building a pot with, and how much to charge.

The Core ISO Range: Hands That Always Qualify

The baseline iso-raise range from the BTN or CO against a single limper is wider than most players expect. Any hand you'd open from HJ or CO in a normal full-ring game belongs here: TT+, AJs+, AQo+, KQs. These hands have strong equity against a single caller and play well heads-up on most board textures.

Below that tier, the decision depends on how many limpers are in and where you're sitting. 88-99, ATs-AJs, KJs+ are clean iso-raises with one or two passive limpers from late position. Suited connectors like 76s, 87s, T9s are profitable from the BTN against a single limper because they have the multiway equity to pay off when they connect and the fold equity on dry boards when they don't. Offsuit broadways — KJo, QJo — weaken fast as limpers multiply; you make second-best hands too often in five-way pots.

A practical tier breakdown for BTN vs 1-2 limpers at $1/$3:

  • Always raise: TT+, AJs+, AQo+, KQs
  • Raise with 1-2 passive limpers: 88-99, ATs, KJs, QJs, JTs, T9s
  • Raise with 1 limper only: 76s-98s, 65s, A9o-ATo in position
  • Fold vs multiple limpers: KJo, QJo, offsuit Ax below ATo

How Opponent Type Reshapes Your Range

The tiers above assume a generic limper. At a live table, you almost always know more. Opponent type is the biggest input after hand strength — and it shifts which hands cross the profitability threshold.

Against a Loose Passive or Calling Station, widen your value range and tighten speculative hands. A9o becomes a strong iso-raise because you'll often hold the best hand at showdown. Suited connectors like 65s drop in value — this opponent calls flop and turn bets mechanically, so you need actual showdown equity rather than fold equity to realize your edge.

Against a Nit, the math flips. Nits limp their weaker Ax hands and small pocket pairs; their limp range is capped. Iso-raising with 76s or T9s in position is excellent because when a Nit continuation bets a dry board, you can float or raise with credibility. Value-bet thin on J-high boards — Nits rarely bluff-catch.

Sizing the Iso-Raise: Dollars Over Ratios

At $1/$3 with one limper, the standard iso-raise is $12-$15 — add $3 per additional limper. That's a 4-5x raise. This is larger than what balanced frameworks suggest because live cash players call too wide; the price needs to be big enough to keep the pot heads-up against most of your table.

Never size down to 2.5x or 3x at a loose table. You end up multiway with a hand that doesn't want to be there. The extra $3-$5 in raise size isn't about fold equity alone — it controls the stack-to-pot ratio so you're comfortable betting three streets if you connect. At $2/$5, the same structure applies in larger numbers: $20-$25 with one limper, add $5 per extra caller. At $5/$10, $40+ is standard.
