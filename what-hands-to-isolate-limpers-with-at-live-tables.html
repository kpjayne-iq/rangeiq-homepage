What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

The right hands for an isolation raise depend on who you're raising — here's how to match your range to the opponent type and limper count.

By RangeIQ Poker · August 2026 · 5 min read

The question is not whether to isolate limpers — it is which hands justify the raise and how to size it. Live cash games at $1/$3 and $2/$5 regularly offer two, three, or even four limpers before you act on the button. Picking the wrong hand to isolate limpers with, or sizing too small, turns a high-EV spot into a leak. This article breaks down which hands to use, how the limper's opponent type shifts that selection, and when to skip the iso entirely.

The Core Logic of an Isolation Raise

An isolation raise accomplishes two things: it gets you heads-up against a player whose range is weak and wide, and it builds the pot while you hold a positional advantage postflop. Both pieces matter. If you just wanted value, you could limp behind. The raise forces out the players behind you and inflates the pot before the flop.

With a single limper at $1/$3, any hand in roughly the top 25% of combinations is a candidate. That covers all pocket pairs down to 55, suited broadways (KTs and up), suited aces (A2s and up), and off-suit broadways down to KTo. As soon as a second limper enters, the math shifts. Suited connectors and small pairs need implied odds to cover the times you miss, and multi-way pots deliver those odds less reliably than heads-up pots. In practice, tighten to approximately the top 15% once three or more players are already in.

Position matters as much as hand strength. Isolating from the cutoff with KJo against a single UTG limper puts you in position against one opponent. Isolating from UTG+1 with the same hand and two callers likely to come in behind puts you in a bloated multi-way pot out of position. The hand does not change; the EV does.

Adjusting Your Isolation Range by Opponent Type

The limper's opponent type is the single biggest variable in deciding how wide to go and how much to charge.

Against a Recreational player or Calling Station, you want to be the last aggressor as often as possible. These opponent types call too wide preflop and continue too often on the flop with weak hands. Your isolation range expands significantly — ATo, KJo, 77, and suited connectors like J♥T♥ all have strong EV because you will see many showdowns against a range full of dominated hands and weak top pairs. Size up: a $16–$18 raise at $1/$3 is correct because the Calling Station calls regardless of size, so you should maximize the pot when you have the advantage.

IQ Reasoning: A♥J♠ holds 63% equity against a Recreational limping range (any pair 22+, any two suited cards, any broadway). Heads-up in position, the engine locks AJo as a clear iso-raise. Sizing to $15 into $6 is correct for this opponent type — the Recreational's calling frequency is near 100%, so a larger size builds maximum value without folding out a caller who would not fold to a smaller bet anyway. With roughly $280 effective stacks, the resulting pot of $31 creates an SPR near 9, giving you three streets of comfortable value on boards where AJo connects.

Against a Nit, iso-raising is still correct but for a different reason. A Nit limping UTG at $1/$3 is doing it with 88–99, suited connectors they do not want to build pots with, or broadway hands they are uncomfortable three-betting. Their range is not pure trash, but it is beatable with any premium hand or suited broadway. Because the Nit folds to raises more often than average, you pick up the pot preflop at a high rate. Size down slightly — $12–$14 — to keep them in when you want a caller and to avoid losing EV when they fold to a larger raise with hands they might have called a smaller one.

Against a Maniac, iso-raising is a trap with medium-strength hands. The Maniac squeezes behind at a high frequency, turning your KJo into a call-or-fold decision out of position with a hand that does not want to face a three-bet. Tighten your isolation range to hands that play well against a re-raise: AA, KK, QQ, JJ, AKs, AKo. Skip the iso with KQo or 88 unless you are comfortable calling a three-bet and navigating a bloated pot against an aggressive, unpredictable player.

Sizing Rules and When Not to Iso

The standard formula at $1/$3: raise to $3 base plus $3 per limper. One limper means a $12 raise. Two limpers means $15. This keeps your sizing consistent while slightly overcharging, which is fine at live tables where players rarely adjust for pot odds. At $2/$5, shift to $20–$22 with one limper and scale up from there.

When to pass on the iso entirely: stack depth below 30 big blinds argues against speculative hands — fold the suited connectors and raise only premiums. More than four limpers pushes the math sharply against you unless you are on the button with AKs or a big pair. And if you have a short stack relative to the limpers, bloating the pot with a hand that needs implied odds to recover is a structural mistake you do not want to review later.
