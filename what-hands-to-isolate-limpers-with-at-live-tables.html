What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Position and opponent type determine your iso-range — not just hand strength.

By RangeIQ Poker · October 2026 · 5 min read

The question of which hands to isolate limpers with does not have a single answer — it depends on where you are sitting, how many limpers are already in, and what kind of player you are targeting. Most $1/$3 regulars either iso too tight (giving up equity by folding hands that dominate the field) or too wide (iso-raising speculative hands from early position with no fold equity and multiple players left to act). This article breaks down how to calibrate your isolation range by position and opponent type.

Why Position Outweighs Hand Strength in Isolation Decisions

When you iso-raise from the button or cutoff, you get to play the entire hand in position against a player who has already signaled a weak range by limping. That positional advantage compresses the hand-strength requirement. From the button against a single Loose Passive limper, hands like K9s, Q10s, J9s, and small pairs down to 44 are all profitable iso-raises at $1/$3 with effective stacks of $200 or deeper. You are not iso-raising these because they are premium — you are raising them because you will consistently outplay the limper on most flop textures.

From early position (UTG or UTG+1), the calculus flips. You still have four to eight players left to act, and any of them can 3-bet you or overcall to create a multi-way pot where your positional edge evaporates. In early position, trim your iso range to hands that have intrinsic value even in multi-way, out-of-position pots: big pairs, broadway combinations like AKs through AJs and KQs, and nothing weaker unless the table is unusually passive. Iso-raising Q9o from UTG+1 in a $1/$3 game is a leak.

Adjusting the Range for Opponent Type

The limper's tendencies matter as much as your position. Against a Nit, even a CO iso-raise should be tighter — Nits limp strong hands they intend to call with, so the fold equity you normally rely on is reduced. Widen your iso range against Recreational players and Loose Passive types who limp-call with dominated hands and will pay off two streets when you hit top pair.

The limper's range is capped — they rarely limp-call with AK or QQ — so KJo is ahead of most of their calling range. At a 5x raise ($15), you price out dominated connectors while keeping the pot manageable. In position post-flop, you can profitably barrel any king-high or jack-high board for two streets. The engine weights this as a clear raise rather than a call because calling surrenders your positional advantage and invites more players into the pot behind you.

Against a Maniac who is likely to limp-raise you, iso-raise only hands that can comfortably call a 3-bet: pairs TT+ and suited broadway hands with strong redraw equity. Iso-raising T9s from the HJ against a known Maniac in the blinds sets you up for an awkward decision when they re-pop it to $60.

Sizing Your Isolation Raise

At most $1/$3 live tables, the correct iso-raise size is 4x to 5x the big blind plus one big blind per additional limper. One limper: raise to $15–$18. Two limpers: raise to $18–$21. This sizing serves two purposes: it charges draws and weak speculative hands enough to make their calls -EV in the long run, and it builds a pot that is large enough to make your post-flop bets meaningful on the flop and turn.

Resist the urge to raise smaller because you are "out of position" or "unsure about your hand." A $10 iso-raise into a $4 pot invites four callers. A $18 raise into a $6 pot usually narrows the field to one or two. The whole point of isolating is to get heads-up in position against the weakest player at the table — a small raise defeats that purpose.

Key takeaway: From late position against passive limpers, iso-raise with any hand you would open-raise plus suited connectors and small pairs. From early position, cut the range to premium hands only. Always use 4–5x sizing plus one big blind per additional limper.
