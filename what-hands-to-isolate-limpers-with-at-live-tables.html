What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Isolation raises are one of the highest-leverage plays at a live $1/$3 table — but only with the right hands against the right opponent types.

By RangeIQ Poker · October 2026 · 5 min read

Most players at $1/$3 and $2/$5 live cash games have an isolation raise problem: they either raise too many hands into limped pots without thinking about who is limping, or they pass on spots where an iso raise is clearly the highest-value action. The right approach is neither. Isolate limpers when your hand dominates their calling range, you are in position, and the opponent type in front of you has a predictable calling or folding tendency. Everything else is situational.

What Makes a Hand Worth Isolating

An isolation raise has one job: get heads-up against a weaker player while building the pot with a hand that plays well in that spot. That means hands that dominate likely calling hands are the best iso candidates. At $1/$3, a limper's calling range is usually something like A9, KT, weak suited aces, and small pairs. You want to be raising with hands that are either ahead of that range or have strong blockers to it.

The strongest iso hands at $1/$3 are TT+, AJ+, KQs, and ATs. These hands hit the top of the limper's calling range hard. A-rag hands like A8 suited are borderline — they play well in position but you are in a dominated scenario more often than you think. QJo and KJo are viable iso hands against Calling Stations but not against tighter limpers where you risk running into KQ or AJ.

The hands that look like iso candidates but are not: suited connectors (87s, 76s, 65s). These hands want three or more opponents and deep implied odds. An iso raise narrows the field to one or two players and shrinks the pot relative to the stack-to-pot ratio on later streets. You earn more with 87s by limping behind and flopping a flush draw against four players than by raising to $15 and going heads-up against an unknown's top-pair hand.

How Opponent Type Changes Your Isolation Range

The limper's opponent type is the most important variable in the iso decision. A Calling Station limping UTG is not the same spot as a Nit limping UTG. Against a Calling Station, you can widen to KJo, QTs, and 99, because their calling range is wide and inelastic — they are not adjusting to your size. Against a Nit, you should iso only with TT+ and AK-AQ, because a Nit limping UTG often has a marginal hand like 55 or A9 that is looking to set-mine or limp-fold, and they are more capable of folding pre or making a disciplined laydown post-flop.

Against a Recreational player, iso wider — any suited broadway hand, any pair 88+, and even offsuit broadways like KJo are fine. Recreational limpers at $1/$3 often have random holdings (J6s, 94o) that cannot continue profitably against any continuation bet. Size up to $18-22 with one limper; they will call. Against a Young Aggro player limping, be cautious — this player may be trapping or looking for a squeeze opportunity, so isolating with speculative hands like 99 or ATs puts you in an uncomfortable spot when they 3-bet you.

Sizing Your Isolation Raise Correctly

At $1/$3, the standard isolation size is 4x the big blind plus 1 BB per additional limper. One limper: raise to $15. Two limpers: $18. Three limpers: $21. Do not size down to $10-12 trying to be “efficient” — a smaller size invites the field to call and turns your heads-up iso attempt into a 3-way pot. You are raising to reduce the field, not to extract tiny amounts of equity from the limpers.

At $2/$5, the same structure applies but calibrate for the calling tendencies at your specific table. If your table is calling-heavy, size up to 5x plus limpers ($25-30 with one limper). If the limpers are marginally capable and fold to reasonable raises, $20-22 with one limper is sufficient. Oversizing at $2/$5 against thinking players gives away information about your hand strength — keep it proportional.
