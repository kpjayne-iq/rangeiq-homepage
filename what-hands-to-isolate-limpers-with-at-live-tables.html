What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Your iso range at a live cash game is not fixed — it shifts based on position, stack depth, and the opponent type you're targeting.

By RangeIQ Poker · October 2026 · 5 min read

Isolation raising is one of the highest-leverage preflop tools in live cash — but most players apply a fixed range regardless of position, stack depth, or who they're targeting. The right iso range at a live $1/$3 or $2/$5 table is not a static hand list. It shifts based on three variables: where you're sitting relative to the limper, the opponent type, and effective stack depth.

Opponent Type Determines Whether You Isolate at All

Not every limper is worth building a pot against. The purpose of an isolation raise is to manufacture a heads-up situation against a player whose post-flop tendencies you can exploit — and that calculus changes completely based on who you're targeting.

Calling Stations and Loose Passive players are the ideal iso targets. Their limp ranges are wide, they call raises with speculative holdings, and they will pay off strong hands through the river. Against these players, your iso range from the button or cutoff widens considerably: Axs, medium pocket pairs, suited connectors 65s and above, any broadway combination. You're not looking for premium hands. You're looking for hands that build strong pairs, sets, and draws on a variety of flop textures, knowing your opponent will call you down.

Nits are a different story. A Nit who limps often holds a small pair or a suited connector they planned to limp-call — their limp range is stronger than average, not weaker. Isolating a Nit with AJo at $1/$3 doesn't exploit weakness; it builds a pot against someone who has real equity on many textures. Against Nits, iso ranges should be tight: AQ+, TT+, KQs in position. Anything less and the edges are too thin.

Young Aggro and LAG players who limp frequently are setting up limp-reraises. A single limp from a known aggro at $2/$5 may be designed to trap you into a pot they built for themselves.

The Calling Station's limp range includes any Ax, most suited hands, and broadway combinations — all of which you dominate when you hit top pair. A♣6♣ adds backdoor flush equity and a nut flush draw on any club board. Raise to $12 — a 4x raise that prices out trash while Calling Stations consistently call. Avoid raising to $18; it makes the pot unwieldy without the corresponding hand strength.

Position Contracts Your Range Out of the Blinds

The second filter is position. From the button or cutoff, you'll have post-flop position on any heads-up pot you create — which means you can profitably iso with a wider range because you'll control pot size and extract value over multiple streets.

From the small blind, you're immediately out of position against both the limper and a potential BB caller. Your iso range from the SB should contract significantly: AJ+, KQs, 99+. You want hands with strong inherent equity that can withstand a multi-way pot if the BB comes along. Speculative hands — 76s, A5s — lose the majority of their value when you're acting first on every post-flop street.

Key takeaway: The same hand that's a profitable iso from the button against a Calling Station can be a clear fold from the small blind against the same player.

Stack Depth Governs Speculative Iso Hands

Effective stack depth is the third variable. At $1/$3 with 100bb ($300) effective, hands like 76s or A5s can profitably iso from position against a Calling Station — the implied odds are large enough to justify the preflop investment. If you connect with a flush draw, two pair, or a set, there's enough money behind to extract over multiple streets.

At $2/$5 with 60bb ($300) effective, those same hands lose their value. You need at least 80–100bb behind to play small suited connectors profitably as isolation hands. When stacks are shallower, weight your range toward higher-equity hands: AJ+, KQ, 99+.

The general rule: the deeper the effective stacks relative to your iso raise size, the wider you can profitably isolate. But this always pairs with position — deep stacks out of position against an aggressive player is not a license to iso wider. It's a reason to play tighter, clearer hands.
