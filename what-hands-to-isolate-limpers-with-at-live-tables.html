What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Your iso-raise range is not fixed — it shifts based on who limped, how many followed, and what position you hold.

By RangeIQ Poker · September 2026 · 5 min read

Isolating limpers is one of the highest-frequency spots in live cash poker, and most players handle it on feel. The question is not whether to raise — it is which hands are worth raising, at what size, and against which limper. The answers change based on position, stack depth, and the opponent type sitting in the pot.

Why the Limper's Opponent Type Rewrites Your Range

A limp does not mean the same thing from every player. A Nit limping from early position typically holds a speculative hand they do not want to bloat the pot with — think 33, 55, or suited connectors. A Loose Passive or Calling Station may limp with any two cards, which collapses fold equity but expands your value range. A Recreational player limps with a wide and unpredictable range that sometimes includes strong hands they're slow-playing.

Against a Nit: tighten your iso range considerably. Their limp-call range is still playable and they will not make costly post-flop mistakes. Hands that need fold equity — KJs, QTs — work better against players who can actually fold.

Against a Calling Station or Loose Passive: your iso range widens, but it shifts toward hands with strong made-hand potential. ATo, KQo, 99, TT all become clear raises. Suited connectors are worth less because your fold equity is near zero — the value comes from flopping the best hand and getting paid across multiple streets.

Against a Recreational player: raise with a broad range of hands that connect well with mid boards. Hands like A5s, T9s, and 87s gain value because the Recreational type calls and continues with dominated holdings.

Sizing the Isolation Raise

The standard live iso-raise mistake is raising too small. At $1/$3 with one limper, raising to $10 often generates two or three additional callers from the blinds, turning a profitable heads-up spot into a multiway pot. The correct size is typically $14–$18 (between 4.5x and 6x the big blind), scaled up by $3–$4 for each additional limper already in.

The goal is not just to charge — it is to reduce the field. A heads-up pot against a Calling Station with KQs is far more valuable than a four-way pot with the same hand.

This opponent type calls raises at high frequency but does not re-raise aggressively, making $18 the correct size to build heads-up value while pricing out the blinds. Post-flop, position compounds the edge — KJs can bet for thin value, take free cards, and apply pressure on brick turns with a single barrel.

Hands to Remove From Your Iso Range

Not every hand that plays well in a multiway limped pot is worth iso-raising. Suited connectors like 65s and 76s need multiway action to realize their draw equity — they perform better called behind in a limped pot than as the raiser building a heads-up pot against a calling range. Similarly, small pairs (22–44) have set-mining value in a multiway pot but limited heads-up equity when they miss, and against Calling Stations they rarely get enough folds on missed flops to justify the raise.

The key principle: iso-raise hands that benefit from a smaller pot with position, not hands that need a crowd.
