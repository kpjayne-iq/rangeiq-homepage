What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

Your iso-raising range should change based on who limped — here is how to set the right threshold against each opponent type.

By RangeIQ Poker · August 2026 · 5 min read

The iso-raise is one of the highest-leverage plays in live cash games, but most players apply the same range regardless of who limped. That is a mistake. The correct hands to isolate limpers with depend almost entirely on who limped and why — and getting that wrong is what turns a profitable spot into a breakeven one.

Why the Limper's Identity Determines Your Range

When you iso-raise a limper, you are doing two things: building a pot in position against a capped range, and getting heads-up with someone who has already shown weakness. But the value you extract depends on what the limper does after the flop, and that is dictated by their opponent type.

Against a Calling Station or Loose Passive, your iso-raising range should tighten toward value hands — top pair or better on most boards — because they will call you down on three streets with weak pairs and draws. You want strong made hands that can bet all three streets without worrying about protection. Adding speculative hands like suited connectors loses value here because you rely on fold equity you will never get.

Against a Nit, the calculus reverses. A Nit who limps under the gun at $1/$3 has a range of hands they are ready to fold to pressure. Here you can profitably iso-raise with a much wider range — suited broadways, small pocket pairs, even suited aces — because your raise will win the pot pre-flop a significant portion of the time, and the few times they call, you are playing a heads-up pot in position against a range they have already capped by not 3-betting.

Against a Recreational player who limps frequently, treat them more like a Calling Station: weight your iso range toward hands that make strong top pairs or better on typical boards — A-x suited, K-Q, pocket pairs 77+. You want to be betting for value on most flops, not semi-bluffing with hands that need the board to cooperate.

A Concrete Example: Adjusting on the Fly

You are on the button at a $1/$3 table. UTG limps for $3. Your default iso range might start at hands like A-J+, K-Q, pocket pairs 88+, and suited connectors 87s+. But stop — who is UTG?

Against a Loose Passive who calls iso-raises wide and continues on most flops, K-T offsuit has no clean value line — you will frequently face a call on flops where top pair is second-best, and you have no fold equity when you miss. The engine assigns 78% confidence to folding because this opponent type converts your speculative iso into a high-variance guessing game with a negative chip EV. Raise to $12-$15 only if you upgrade to hands that make strong top pair on A, K, or T boards — or drop to suited connectors that can profitably semi-bluff fold equity they will occasionally provide.

Now change UTG to a Nit who you have seen fold to pre-flop 3-bets four times in the last two hours. K-T suited or K-T offsuit both become profitable iso-raises because you are winning the pot outright often enough, and when called, you are rarely dominated. The iso raise becomes a simple bet-fold on most flops, using the Nit's postflop passivity against them.

Sizing, Position, and Multi-Way Caution

At $1/$3, iso-raise to $12-$15 from the button when there is one limper — roughly 4x the big blind plus $3 per limper. Against a Calling Station, lean toward the larger end ($15-$18) to make their implied odds calls less automatic. Against a Nit, $12 is often enough to accomplish what you need.

From earlier positions — UTG+1 or HJ — tighten your iso range by at least a tier regardless of opponent type, because you will often play the rest of the hand out of position. Hands like J-Ts or 65s that play well in position become marginal iso candidates from early position; fold them and wait for a spot where you hold the positional advantage.

If there are already two or three limpers, the pot odds and implied equity shift the math significantly. A single iso raise is unlikely to get everyone to fold. In those spots, either tighten to premium pairs and broadways that play well multi-way, or skip the iso entirely and look for a better spot. Multi-way pots reduce the exploitability of limpers because the raw equity math starts to dominate over the opponent-type adjustments.

Session review tip: After your next live session, flag every iso-raise you made and note who the limper was. If you iso-raised the same hands against a Nit and a Calling Station, you have found a leak worth studying.
