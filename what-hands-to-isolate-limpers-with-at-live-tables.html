What Hands to Isolate Limpers With at Live Tables

The hands that profit from isolation, the sizing that builds the right pot, and how opponent type changes the calculus.

By RangeIQ Poker · September 2026 · 5 min read

Isolating limpers is one of the highest-leverage plays available at live $1/$3 and $2/$5. When one or two players limp in front of you, a raise from late position puts them in a bad spot: they face a bigger pot out of position with a hand they already told you is marginal. The question most players get wrong is not whether to isolate, but which hands are actually worth it and how to size the raise so the math works in your favor.

Which Hands Warrant an Isolation Raise

Not every hand you would open from the button is worth iso-raising a limper. The baseline shifts because you are now building a larger pot against a player who is almost certainly weak — but you still need a hand that can barrel multiple streets and win at showdown.

Strong iso hands (raise every time): Pocket pairs TT and above, AK, AQ, KQs. These hands want a big pot against a weak range and play well in position regardless of what flops.

Conditional iso hands (raise against weak limper types, fold against tight limpers): Pocket pairs 77–99, AJs, ATs, KJs, QJs, JTs. These hands have good playability and implied odds against a Loose Passive or Calling Station but become marginal when a Nit limps in early position, where their range is capped to strong pairs and suited broadways fishing for a cheap flop.

Hands to fold or flat (almost never isolate): Suited aces below AT, KTo, QTo, and most small pairs below 77. These hands do not have the raw equity or postflop playability to justify a larger pot from out of position or a thin position advantage.

How to Size the Iso Raise

The standard formula at most $1/$3 and $2/$5 games is 3x the big blind plus 1x per limper. With one limper at $1/$3, that is $12. With two limpers, $15. This sizing accomplishes two things: it charges enough to make marginal calls unprofitable for their range, and it builds a pot you can bet twice on most flops without going absurd on bet size relative to stack depth.

Where players deviate — and give up edge — is by going too small. A raise to $8 into a $1/$3 limped pot gets three callers, destroys position value, and turns your QJs into a multiway guess. If your goal is to play a heads-up pot in position, the raise has to be big enough to actually accomplish that.

Against a Calling Station, you can shade slightly larger — $16 instead of $12 — because they will call anyway. You are not pricing anyone out; you are just building a bigger pot to value-bet on later streets.

Sizing rule: 3x the big blind plus 1x per limper. Adjust up by $2–$4 when you know the limper calls wide and you have a strong value hand.

Against a Recreational player who never folds to raises and never 3-bets, you want to isolate wider and size up.
